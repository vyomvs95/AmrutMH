0.  WHAT YOU ARE BUILDING
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Build a NEW website at ~/Amrut_Manus (create it) — a second, parallel
design of the Government of Maharashtra's AMRUT portal. There is already a
first version at ~/Amrut_Maharashtra. DO NOT modify that one. Read from it,
copy assets out of it, and otherwise leave it alone.

This version has a different thesis. Where version one is a well-organised
news portal, this one is a photo-documentary publication: Humans of New
York's storytelling format, carried inside Ford Foundation's navigation and
institutional restraint.

The organisation: AMRUT — महाराष्ट्र संशोधन, उन्नती व प्रशिक्षण प्रबोधिनी —
an autonomous body of the Government of Maharashtra. It serves economically
weaker households in open-category communities that receive no other
government corporation's benefit: 24 listed castes, annual household income
under ₹8 lakh, offices in all 36 districts. The audience is largely rural,
Marathi-speaking, on inexpensive Android phones and unreliable data.

Marathi is the first language of this site. Not a translation of an English
site — the English is the afterthought, if it exists at all.

Deliver it deployed. Not a mockup, not a plan. Deployed, on a live URL,
verified working.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
4.  DESIGN DIRECTION
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

REFERENCE A — Humans of New York (the storytelling format)
  One person. One photograph, large. Their words. No competing furniture.
  Stories arrive one at a time and you scroll through lives, not a grid of
  cards. Take the FORMAT and the pacing.

REFERENCE B — Ford Foundation (navigation and institutional bearing)
  A confident full-overlay menu with generous space and real typographic
  hierarchy. Motion that is orchestrated rather than sprinkled. The feeling
  that a serious institution made this. Take the NAVIGATION, the SPACING,
  and the RESTRAINT.

  Do not copy either site. Take the thinking, make your own thing.

⚠ THE CONSTRAINT THAT WILL BITE YOU — read this before designing

  HONY works because every post is the subject's own first-person voice.
  These articles are written in third-person official register: "कुलकर्णी
  यांनी अमृत संस्थेचे मनापासून आभार मानले आहेत."

  You may NOT rewrite the articles. Content is fixed.

  I checked: only 8 of the 32 full articles contain direct quoted speech,
  and some of those are historical quotations or product names, not
  testimony. So roughly 1 in 5 stories can carry a genuine first-person
  pull quote.

  Handle it like this:
    · Where quoted Marathi speech of 40+ characters exists in the body,
      extract it and set it as a large display pull quote with attribution.
      That IS the HONY moment and it is real.
    · Where it does not, fall back to the article's own `summary` field,
      set as a standfirst — clearly editorial, NOT dressed up as a quote.
    · Never fabricate a quote. Never attribute words to a person that they
      are not recorded as saying. This is a government site about real,
      named, poor people. Getting this wrong is the worst thing you could do.

⚠ THE PHOTOGRAPHY CEILING — the real limit on this design

  The source imagery is uneven: group shots of twenty people holding
  certificates under fluorescent light, GPS-camera watermarks burned into
  frames, event posters photographed as images, mixed aspect ratios.

  A full-bleed portrait format is merciless with weak photography. Design
  around it:
    · A strong scrim/gradient from the ink colour over every hero image.
      It makes an ordinary frame read as deliberate and guarantees text
      legibility over anything.
    · Detect portrait vs landscape from images.json (width/height) and
      choose the crop and layout per image rather than forcing one ratio.
    · Prefer images whose aspect suits the slot; fall back gracefully.
    · Duotone or a warm colour-grade over weak frames is legitimate and
      will unify the library considerably. Use canvas or CSS blend modes.

TYPOGRAPHY — Google Fonts only, and it must be built for Devanagari
  Display / story text:  "Tiro Devanagari Marathi"  (a Marathi-specific serif)
  Interface / metadata:  "Mukta"  (Ek Type, Mumbai)
  Optional third for large display: "Anek Devanagari" (variable) or
  "Rozha One" (high-contrast, use sparingly)

  Devanagari typesetting rules — most sites get these wrong, do not:
    · Body line-height 1.85–1.95, NOT the 1.5 a Latin face takes. Matras
      sit above and below the shirorekha and need the room.
    · Letter-spacing 0 or slightly negative. Never positive — it breaks
      the headline stroke.
    · NEVER uppercase. It does not exist in Devanagari, and Latin caps set
      beside it read as a foreign object. Use size, weight and colour for
      hierarchy instead.
    · Reading measure ~34rem (~62 Devanagari characters).

COLOUR DISCIPLINE
  The live site runs saffron at full strength on the header, nav, buttons,
  headings and links simultaneously, so nothing leads. Keep the exact same
  hues and cut saffron to roughly 5% of the surface — spend it once per
  screen, on the single thing you most want the reader to do.

  Light theme only, committed and painted explicitly. The cream-and-saffron
  identity does not survive inversion and this is read in daylight.

5.  MOTION — the brief asks for this to be graphical and alive
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Rules that override everything below:
  · Transform and opacity only. No layout-thrashing properties.
  · No animation library. CSS + IntersectionObserver + rAF is enough.
  · EVERY effect must be disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`,
    and the page must still be complete and correct without it.
  · The audience is on cheap Android hardware. If an effect costs frames,
    cut it. Test that scrolling stays smooth.

Build these:

  1. OPENING SEQUENCE (once per session, sessionStorage-gated)
     Saffron on ink. An octagonal seal in the Rajmudra tradition drawing
     itself via stroke-dashoffset, a radiating sun fanning out, then the
     अमृत महाराष्ट्र wordmark rising. ~2.5s, then fade.
     ⚠ Stylised interpretation only — the real राजमुद्रा carries a specific
     Sanskrit shloka and hand-setting it risks getting a revered
     inscription wrong. Leave a clean seam to drop official artwork in.

  2. STORY FEED — the signature interaction
     Each story occupies a full viewport section. As you scroll:
       · the photograph parallaxes slowly against the text
       · the pull quote / standfirst rises and fades in, staggered by line
       · the previous story's image dims and desaturates as it leaves
     Use IntersectionObserver, not scroll listeners, for the triggers.

  3. NAVIGATION OVERLAY (Ford Foundation register)
     Full-screen. Section names in large display type, staggered entrance
     ~45ms apart, grouped by the three strands. A live scheme panel pinned
     in the corner. Escape closes. Focus is trapped while open.

  4. NUMBER COUNTERS
     Article counts and district counts animate up on entry.
     ⚠ CRITICAL: a stalled counter shows a FALSE number. rAF stops in a
     background tab. So: never leave the true value until the animation
     actually begins, always set a hard timeout that lands the real figure,
     and snap to it on visibilitychange. The live site's fake visitor
     counter is the single worst thing about it — do not ship a number
     that can lie.

  5. IMAGE ENTRANCE
     Fade + slight scale-down from a warm placeholder. Check
     `img.complete` on mount or cached images stay invisible forever.

  6. SCROLL PROGRESS
     A hairline saffron bar at the top of the viewport.

  7. MAGNETIC / LIFT HOVER
     Cards and scheme tiles lift 4px on hover with a soft shadow.
     Pointer-fine media queries only — do not do this on touch.

  8. ROUTE TRANSITION
     Content fades and rises ~10px on navigation. Keyed on pathname.

  9. DISTRICT MAP (if you have the appetite)
     /districts with an SVG Maharashtra outline, districts we have stories
     from lighting up in saffron on hover, the rest muted. Purely
     decorative enhancement over an accessible list — the list must work
     with the SVG absent.

7.  TECHNICAL
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  Vite + React 19 + Tailwind 4 + React Router 7. Plain JS, not TypeScript.
  vercel.json with an SPA rewrite: /(.*) → /index.html
  <html lang="mr">, real per-page <title> and meta description in Marathi.
  Generate robots.txt and sitemap.xml at build (read the real production
  domain from VERCEL_PROJECT_PRODUCTION_URL, do not hard-code a guess).
  Open Graph tags — WhatsApp is how this content actually travels.
  Code-split the article route; the 32 bodies are ~255 KB and a homepage
  visitor should never load them.
  Images: srcset across 400/800/1400, lazy below the fold, explicit
  width/height to prevent layout shift.
  Accessibility: skip link, visible focus states, semantic landmarks, alt
  text, keyboard-operable overlay with focus trapping, no keyboard traps.

TRAPS ALREADY HIT ON VERSION ONE — do not rediscover these

  · Unlayered CSS beats Tailwind utilities. A bare `a { color: inherit }`
    silently kills every colour utility on links. Base element styles go in
    @layer base, custom classes in @layer components.
  · Cached images never fire onLoad. Check `ref.current.complete` on mount
    or anything in cache sits at opacity 0 forever.
  · React StrictMode double-invokes effects in dev. Write "already seen"
    flags when a sequence FINISHES, not when it starts, or the second pass
    skips it.
  · A catch-all `/:catSlug` route swallows unknown URLs. Category and
    Article must render <NotFound />, not redirect home, or the 404 is
    unreachable.
  · Chrome auto-translates the site to English because lang="mr". Inject
    <meta name="google" content="notranslate"> before taking screenshots.
  · Scheme banners are 2.5:1 with text baked in. Any other crop cuts words.

10.  HOW TO JUDGE WHETHER IT WORKED
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  A farmer in Jalna opens this on a ₹8,000 phone over 3G. It loads fast.
  He recognises a person like himself in the first photograph. He reads a
  story in Marathi that is set well enough that reading is pleasant. At
  the end, instead of being told to go find a district office, he is shown
  the exact scheme that story was about, what it takes to qualify, and a
  way in.

  That is the whole test. Everything else is in service of it.
