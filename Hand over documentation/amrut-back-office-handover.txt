================================================================================
AMRUT MAHARASHTRA
BACK OFFICE — HANDOVER DOCUMENTATION
What it does, who uses it, and how to run and change it
================================================================================

Prepared        : 9 October 2026
For             : AMRUT Maharashtra and their development team
Covers          : the staff side of amrutmaharashtra.org — the system where
                  district coordinators write stories, the divisional head
                  checks them, and the head office publishes
Built with      : Node.js and PostgreSQL 18
Lives in        : the "server" folder of the project repository
Reaches the site: through a read-only path at /api, which the public portal uses

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT THIS IS, IN ONE PARAGRAPH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The public portal is the shop window. This is the office at the back. Staff sign
in at a separate address, write and check stories, sell advertising space, and
watch how much is being read. Nothing here is visible to a visitor, and the
portal itself only ever reads what has been published.

It is a self-contained folder. Copy it to a server, fill in five lines of
settings, and it runs. It needs one outside package in production.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE THREE LEVELS OF OFFICER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

These are the levels AMRUT described: representatives in all 36 districts,
a divisional head above every group of districts, and the head office above them.

2.1 District coordinator — जिल्हा समन्वयक
  Signs in with their own e-mail and password.
  Sees only their own district. Another district's story returns "not found",
  as does its photograph.
  Can: write a story, attach up to 8 photographs, save it as a draft, edit it,
  send it for review; record an advertisement they have sold and enter the money
  they collected; see how much their district is being read.
  Cannot: approve anything, publish anything, or see other districts.

2.2 Divisional head — विभागीय प्रमुख
  Sees every district in their division. (The brief said six districts each; in
  practice Maharashtra's six divisions hold 7, 5, 5, 8, 5 and 6. The system is
  seeded with the real figures.)
  Can: read every story in the division, approve one, or send it back with a
  written reason; see the division's advertising and reading figures.
  Cannot: write a story, publish, or manage accounts.

2.3 Head office editor — मुख्य कार्यालय संपादक
  Sees the whole state.
  Can: read and edit any story; publish it — this is the only level that can;
  send a story back; check an advertisement's payment and start it running; stop
  one; see every account; see all reading and advertising figures.
  Cannot: nothing is withheld, except writing a story as a district (by design —
  stories belong to a district).

2.4 How signing in works today
  E-mail and password at  <site address>/office  — for example
  https://amrutmaharashtra.org/office
  The password is stored scrambled (scrypt with a per-password salt), never in
  readable form. A sign-in lasts 12 hours. Eight wrong attempts for the same
  e-mail and address within 15 minutes blocks further tries for a while.
  A wrong e-mail and a wrong password give exactly the same message, so the
  office cannot be used to discover who has an account.

  NOTE FOR AMRUT: this is a login of its own. If the common sign-on across your
  22 sites replaces it, we need your authentication documentation; the change is
  contained to one file (src/core/auth.js) and the sign-in page.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE TABS, ONE BY ONE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 मुख्यपृष्ठ · Home
  The first screen after signing in. Two rows of figures: stories by state
  (drafts, awaiting review, published, sent back) and advertising (running now,
  awaiting approval, money collected, and — for a coordinator — their own count).
  Below them, a plain list of what this account is allowed to do.
  Every figure respects the signed-in person's level.

3.2 बातम्या · Stories
  The list of stories this person may see, ten at a time.
  Sort by any column — headline, district, section, state or date — by clicking
  the heading; click again to reverse it. Headlines sort in Marathi alphabetical
  order, not by byte value.
  Search by headline in the box at the top right.
  A district coordinator also gets "+ नवीन बातमी लिहा" to write a new one.

3.3 तपासणीसाठी · Review   (divisional head and head office only)
  The same list, narrowed to what is waiting on this person: stories submitted
  for the divisional head, and submitted or approved for the head office.

3.4 जाहिराती · Advertising
  Every advertisement this person may see, sortable the same way.
  A link to the rate card. A district coordinator also gets
  "+ नवीन जाहिरात नोंदवा" to record one they have sold.

3.5 आकडेवारी · Analytics
  Described in full in section 6.

3.6 बातमीपट्टी · Ticker   (head office only)
  The band of six scrolling lines under the portal's navigation.
  The screen shows the band exactly as the portal draws it, then the six lines
  below. One line is edited at a time — its words, its link, and whether it is
  shown. Drag a line to change its place in the order, or use the arrows beside
  it. A link must be a path inside the portal (/govet-schemes) or a full web
  address; anything else is refused.
  The portal picks a change up immediately — no rebuild, no deploy.

3.7 वापरकर्ते · People   (head office only)
  Every account, its level and its district or division. Read-only today.

3.8 माझे खाते · Account
  Your own name, e-mail, level and location.

4. HOW A STORY TRAVELS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  draft ──send for review──► submitted ──approve──► approved ──publish──► published
    ▲                            │                      │
    └─────── sent back ◄─────────┴──────────────────────┘   (with a written reason)

4.1 Writing it
  The coordinator enters a headline, picks the section it belongs to (the same
  16 sections the portal already uses), writes the story, and attaches
  photographs. It saves as a draft and can be edited as often as needed.

4.2 Photographs
  JPG, PNG or WEBP. Up to 8 a story, 12 MB each. Every photograph is checked by
  its actual first bytes, not its file name, so a renamed file cannot slip in.
  Each is kept as it arrived and also written as WebP at 400, 800 and 1400 pixels
  wide — the same three sizes the portal already uses — so a page asks for the
  size it needs instead of a 4 MB camera photograph.

4.3 Checking it
  The divisional head approves it or sends it back with a reason, which the
  coordinator sees on the story. The head office can also publish straight from
  "submitted" without waiting — the brief said audited "either by the divisional
  head or by the head office".

4.4 Publishing it
  Only the head office. The moment it is published it becomes visible to the
  public read path and appears on the portal.

4.5 Everything is recorded
  Created, edited, submitted, approved, sent back, published — each with who did
  it, when, and from which address, in the audit_log table. Sign-ins, failed
  sign-ins and sign-outs are recorded the same way.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. HOW AN ADVERTISEMENT TRAVELS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  recorded ──money entered──► awaiting check ──head office confirms──► running
       │                              │                                   │
       └──────────────────────────────┴── rejected (with a reason)        └── ends

5.1 The placements that can be sold
  Six, each with its pixel size: a popup when the site opens, a slot in the
  rotating hero, a full-width banner, one above the footer, a side banner, and
  one inside a story. Each sellable for 2, 7, 14 or 30 days.

5.2 The rate card
  Price for every placement and duration, shown at जाहिराती → दरपत्रक.
  THE PRICES ARE PLACEHOLDERS. The client's own chart sits with Hemant, and
  AMRUT's 13-15 lakh monthly viewership means the figures need raising. They are
  changed in db/seed-ads.js and are flagged on screen until they are replaced.

5.3 The money
  The coordinator records how much was taken, how it arrived (UPI, bank
  transfer, cheque, cash, or a payment gateway) and the reference number. The
  head office checks the receipt and starts the advertisement. Every rupee is
  recorded with who took it and who checked it.

  NO PAYMENT GATEWAY IS CONNECTED. Every mode is entered by hand, which is how
  coordinators collect today. A gateway plugs into the same step — the system
  already allows "gateway" as a mode — and needs a merchant account and
  compliance sign-off from AMRUT. Nothing else changes when it arrives.

5.4 Running and ending
  An advertisement runs from the day it is confirmed until its days are up, then
  stops by itself. The head office can also stop one early.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. THE DASHBOARD — WHAT IS READ, AND BY WHOM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

6.1 Two numbers, always
  एकूण वाचन · all views     — every read, the same person counted again on a
                              later visit. This is what an advertiser is buying.
  वेगवेगळे वाचक · unique readers — how many different people. This is what AMRUT
                              should plan with.
  Both appear side by side everywhere, with views-per-reader between them.

6.2 How a reader is counted without being identified
  A visitor becomes a short scrambled value made from their address, browser and
  language, mixed with a secret THAT IS REPLACED EVERY DAY. No address, browser
  string or identifier is stored. So the same person counts once a day per story,
  and tomorrow their value is different — nobody, including us, can follow a
  reader from one day to the next. A refresh within a minute does not inflate the
  figures either.

6.3 What is measured
  Both the stories written in the office and the 3,035 stories that came across
  from the old site, so the whole portal is covered. Advertisements carry
  impressions, reach and clicks.

6.4 What is on the screen
  - Four figures: all views, unique readers, views per reader, published in this
    period.
  - A day-by-day bar for each day; point at a bar for that day's figures.
  - By district, by section, and the most-read stories — each sortable, the
    most-read list ten at a time with a search box.
  - Advertising: money collected, impressions, clicks, and a row per
    advertisement with its click rate.
  - Everything on screen downloads as a CSV from the same filters.

6.5 Filters
  Period (7, 30, 90 or 365 days), district, section, and whether a story was
  written in the office or came from the archive. The head office can filter by
  district; the other levels are already limited to their own area.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. WHAT THE PUBLIC PORTAL READS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

An open, read-only path. Published and running things only. No sign-in, and no
staff detail in any reply.

  GET /api/sections                 the 16 sections, with how many stories each
  GET /api/stories?section=&page=   published stories, newest first
  GET /api/stories/<id>             one story in full, with related ones
  GET /api/img/<id>?w=400|800|1400  a story photograph at that width
  GET /api/ads?slot=                advertisements running right now
  GET /api/ads/img/<id>?w=          the artwork of a running advertisement
  GET /api/ticker                   the six scrolling lines, in order
  POST /api/view/<id>               records that a story was read
  POST /api/ads/<id>/click          records a click on an advertisement

A draft, a story in review, an unpaid advertisement and a finished one all
return "not found" here. Each of those cases is covered by an automated test.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. ADDRESSES THAT CANNOT BE GUESSED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

AMRUT asked that nobody be able to count their way from one page to the next.
No address anywhere contains 1, 2, 3. Every record carries its own:

  /office/stories/Xt-QGgImsHYGewqIU5zUBgIlhZ92

That is 16 random bytes followed by a 6-character checksum. The randomness makes
it unguessable; the checksum lets the server throw out a mistyped or invented
address without touching the database. The checksum deliberately uses no secret
key — rotating one would break every address already saved.

A page that does not exist and a page you are not allowed to open return exactly
the same "not found", so the office cannot be used to work out what exists.

9. RUNNING IT ON A LAPTOP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Node.js 22.5 or newer. Check with  node -v

  cd server
  cp .env.example .env
  npm run db:start        # a PostgreSQL just for this machine, no admin rights
                          # copy the DATABASE_URL it prints into .env
  npm install pg
  node db/seed.js         # tables, divisions, districts, sections, 3 accounts
  node db/seed-ads.js     # advertising placements and the rate card
  node db/seed-legacy.js  # names of the 3,035 archive stories (optional)
  npm start

Open  http://localhost:4000/office

db/seed.js prints one starting account per level and writes them to
data/first-logins.txt, which is never committed. Change those passwords before
the office is reachable from the internet.

To work with no database at all, leave DATABASE_URL blank and it falls back to a
single file, data/amrut.db. PostgreSQL is what is tested and what production uses.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. PUTTING IT ON AMRUT'S SERVER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. Copy the "server" folder onto the machine.
  2. Copy .env.example to .env and fill in:
       DATABASE_URL     their PostgreSQL details
       BASE_PATH        where the office sits, e.g. /office
       SECURE_COOKIES   1 once the site is served over https
       PORTAL_URL       the public site, so the office can link out to a story
       ARCHIVE_IMAGE_BASE  where the old site's photographs still live
  3. npm install --omit=dev        (installs only "pg")
  4. node db/setup.js              creates the tables and applies any changes
     node db/seed.js               divisions, districts, sections, first account
     node db/seed-ads.js           placements and rates
  5. node src/server.js, kept running by whatever the server already uses —
     systemd, pm2, or cPanel's Node.js App panel.
  6. Put it behind the existing web server so /office and /api reach this port.

Nothing else changes. The same code runs on the file database on a laptop and on
PostgreSQL in production.

  THE ONE OPEN QUESTION: does the hosting run Node.js, and can a PostgreSQL
  database be created? Ordinary PHP shared hosting often cannot run Node. If the
  answer is no, this needs either a small separate server or a rewrite in PHP.

12. WHERE TO CHANGE WHAT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

I want to change the wording on a screen.
  The module's views.js. Nothing else holds text.
  Example: the story screens are src/modules/editorial/views.js

I want to change who can do what.
  editorial: abilities() and scopeClause() in src/modules/editorial/routes.js
  advertising: abilities() and scope() in src/modules/advertising/routes.js
  These are the only places permissions are decided. Every action re-checks them
  on the server; the buttons on screen are only a hint.

I want to add a column to a table, or a new screen.
  Add the column to the module's views.js and, if it must be sortable, add its
  key to that module's sort map. Sorting only ever happens through those maps,
  so nothing from the address bar reaches the query.

I want to add a whole new section to the office.
  Make a folder under src/modules, write routes.js and views.js, add it to the
  list in src/app.js, and add its tab in src/shared/layout.js. A module exports
  handle(ctx) and returns true once it has answered.

I want to change the database.
  Never edit schema.*.sql for an existing installation. Add a numbered file to
  db/migrations and run  npm run migrate.  Each migration runs inside a
  transaction and is recorded, so it can never half-apply or run twice. If the
  change differs per engine, write two files: 004_x.postgres.sql and
  004_x.sqlite.sql — they are remembered under one name.

I want to change the look.
  src/public/office.css — one file, no frameworks. Its address carries a hash of
  the file, so a change is never served stale from a browser cache.

I want to change the rate card.
  db/seed-ads.js, then re-run it. It only fills in what is missing; existing
  rates are left alone, so change them in the database or clear them first.

I want to change what the portal can read.
  src/modules/publicapi/routes.js. Every query there filters on status. This is
  the file where a mistake would be public — change it with care and run the
  tests.

13. CHECKING NOTHING IS BROKEN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  npm start            in one terminal
  node test/smoke.js   in another

54 checks over real HTTP. It signs in as all three levels, writes a story with a
photograph, takes it through review to publication, sells an advertisement,
records the money, has the head office confirm it, counts reads — and then checks
everything that must NOT be possible: a draft reaching the public, a coordinator
approving their own work, a tampered address, an unpaid advertisement appearing
on the portal, one district opening another's story.

RUN IT AFTER EVERY CHANGE. It is the only thing standing between a small edit
and a quiet mistake.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
14. DEPENDENCIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

One in production: pg, the PostgreSQL connector.

Everything else is built into Node — the web server, password scrambling, reading
photograph uploads, and even a fallback database. This is deliberate: a
government server often cannot reach the internet to install packages, and a
short list is far easier to get through a security review.

Two optional extras:
  sharp             makes the responsive photograph copies. If a locked-down
                    server cannot build it, the office still runs and serves the
                    originals — nothing breaks, the photographs are just heavier.
  embedded-postgres a throwaway PostgreSQL for a laptop. Development only;
                    install production with  npm install --omit=dev

15. SECURITY, FOR THE REVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Passwords stored with scrypt and a per-password salt. Never readable.
  - Sign-in cookies are signed; a tampered one is rejected without a database
    lookup. HttpOnly and SameSite=Lax, and Secure when SECURE_COOKIES=1.
  - Every form carries a one-time field checked against a cookie, so another
    site cannot submit on a signed-in person's behalf.
  - Sign-in limited to 8 attempts per e-mail and address per 15 minutes.
  - A wrong e-mail and a wrong password give the same message.
  - Addresses are unguessable; "not allowed" and "does not exist" look identical.
  - Uploaded photographs are accepted only if their first bytes really are a JPG,
    PNG or WEBP, and are saved under an unguessable name, never the uploaded one.
  - A photograph attached to one district's story cannot be fetched by another.
  - Every office page carries noindex, so it never appears in search results.
  - Sorting and filtering can only use a fixed list of columns; nothing from the
    address bar reaches a query.
  - No reader's address is stored anywhere.
  - Sign-ins, failures and every editorial and advertising action are recorded in
    audit_log.

  Before it faces the public internet: change the starting passwords, serve over
  https with SECURE_COOKIES=1, and let AMRUT's own security audit look at it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
16. WHAT IS NOT BUILT YET
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Known and deliberate. Listed so nobody discovers them in a demonstration.

  Accounts
    No screen to create, edit or switch off an account. The 36 district
    coordinators cannot be created from inside the office today — only by a
    script. THIS IS THE LARGEST GAP.
    Nobody can change their own password, and there is no password reset.

  Stories
    No deleting a story, no captions or reordering of photographs, no edit
    history, no scheduled publishing, no notification when something is waiting.

  Advertising
    No payment gateway and no self-serve path for an outside advertiser to buy a
    banner themselves. No targets per coordinator. No rate-card editing on
    screen. No invoice or receipt, and no GST handling.
    Advertisements are not displayed on the public portal yet — the office serves
    them at /api/ads, but the portal does not ask for them.

  Analytics
    No scheduled e-mail reports, no comparison against the previous period, and
    no traffic sources — that last one needs a referrer, which deserves a privacy
    decision first.

  Everywhere
    Never run on a real server, never over https, never used by a real
    coordinator on a real phone.
